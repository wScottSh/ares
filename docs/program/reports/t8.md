# T8 report: DMA engines as RI bus clients

Status: done, with the check items short of band listed below. Branch feat/t8, head 808e5f54b (origin/master 0eeaa278 with T7a merged in). PR: https://github.com/wScottSh/ares/pull/57 (base master, ready, not merged).

## What changed

- **SP DMA** (`rsp/dma.cpp`). Bursts of at most 128 B (`sp.dma-burst`) never cross a 2 KiB row (`RiBus::split`). One burst is in flight at a time, and the next posts when the previous one's data has moved. The registers advance per grant, so the DMA no longer steps per RSP block or per issue pair (measure-28). A halted RSP is parked even while its DMA runs. `SP_DMA_BUSY` reads busy until the last data beat. `DMA_FULL` clears and the queued transfer starts at the last grant.
- **PI DMA** (`pi/dma.cpp`). The DMA runs block by block:
  - Each block fills from the cart bus at BSD timing: `pi.page-setup` + LAT per page address, and PWD + RLS + `pi.halfword-bias` per halfword.
  - The block then goes to RDRAM as one write burst.
  - The next block starts once that burst lands, plus the PI's share of `pi.block-writeback`. That share is 28 rclk minus the RI wire time and the mean edge wait.
  - `PI_CART_ADDR` advances as a block fills and `PI_DRAM_ADDR` when it lands.
  - The closed `dmaDuration` formula is gone, and both of its known errors go with it (dma-timing.md).
  - `PI::readWord` no longer charges a forced write finish twice.
- **SI DMA** (`si/dma.cpp`). READ64B runs the joybus phase (`pif.estimateTiming`, unchanged), then writes the 64 B through the RI. WRITE64B reads RDRAM through the RI at its start and hands the bytes to the PIF after `si.write64`, or when the read lands if that is later. The PIF's DMA side now takes a byte buffer.
- **AI DMA** (`ai/ai.cpp`). One 8 B read (`ai.fetch-bytes`) covers each stereo pair. The registers and the IRQ keep their sample-time behavior. The host stream gets the pair's frames when the read lands.
- **RI** (`ri/bus.*`).
  - `RiBus::Client` moves to `bus.hpp`, so devices declared before the RI can be clients.
  - `RI::run` moves DMA bursts of any size, in bus byte order.
  - An HSYNC that arrives while a refresh is waiting or running adds no request. A VI programmed CTRL-first (romgen `vi_init`) has 1-VCLK lines until H_SYNC is written. Sixteen queued refreshes then overflowed the channel and starved the CPU in `bench:mi-memset-rspdma`.
- **`ri.overhead-write` refit from 1.7 to 1 rclk.** Grants land on rclk edges, so a 128 B write repeats every 17 + 1 + ceil(overhead) rclk. The old 1.7 rounded every burst up to 20 rclk, which is 6.24 B/rclk.
- **`Rdram::ram` friends.** AI, PI, PIF and RSP are removed. The remaining friends are RI, MI, Loader, and VI and RDP until T11 and T13.
- **Tests and tools.**
  - `unit:ri-split` tests the burst rule.
  - `unit:rdram-private`: a device read of `rdram.ram` must fail on the access check, and a `Loader` read must compile. Making `ram` public fails the test.
  - `n64-run` prints an `ri_dma` line with grants, bytes and wait per engine, and mmbench files it.
- **`tools/n64-timing/pidma-replay.py`.** The pi_dma_test ROM prints its COUNT measurement only for its first 8 failures. The replay rebuilds that measurement for all 24,512 points from `ARES_PILOG`. It then checks each point against the golden logs' min..max.
  - `--calibrate` fits the two code-path shifts to the values the ROM printed in the same run.
  - Validation on feat/t6 and on T8-over-T6: the fit reproduces every printed value exactly.
  - On master with T7a: the fit leaves an error of 1 tick, with the read shift in 144..183.
- Save state version `v153.8-dma`.

## Deviations

1. **No `rsp/actor.hpp`.** SpDma is `RSP::DMA` in `rsp/dma.cpp`. The RSP already was the actor (T5). Its readiness no longer involves DMA, because the Bus's decision time keeps the RSP from passing an undecided burst.
2. **Friend list.** It keeps VI and RDP besides RI, MI and Loader, until T11 and T13. The plan's "RI, debugger, loader" assumes those units have landed.
3. **Files beyond the plan.**
   - `pif/io.cpp` and `pif.hpp` take a byte buffer.
   - `ri/bus.*` gets the client interface, the DMA byte move and the refresh latch.
   - `n64-run`, mmbench, README, checks.tsv, the unit tests and `pidma-replay.py`.
4. **`ri.overhead-write` refit (T6's row).** The value goes from 1.7 to 1 rclk, because the grid quantizes it. The fit's verify-is-fit note stands.
5. **Refresh latch.** It has no hardware reference. At any real line rate it never triggers.
6. **PI per-block split.** The split assumes a row hit and half an rclk of mean edge wait.
7. **AI emits frames at the grant.** Only host audio sees this; it does not change emulated state.

## Follow-ups

- **pidma, small sizes.** The model runs about 12 rclk below the hardware midpoint at every size (inferred: constant across 1-3 blocks). A per-transfer constant of that size would bring the T6-based replay to 23979/24000 (what-if, not applied). No reference names it. It may be CPU-side measurement time, such as `sysad.register-write` (a model-choice of 5 rclk).
- **pidma at RAM 0x7fc/0x7fe.** A first block that ends at the row end costs hardware about 29 rclk more than the model charges. The block's write is empty in ares's functional model.
- **mi-memset-rspdma.** It reads -0.4%. A 128 B write cannot average 19.7 rclk on the rclk grid.
- **bench:si-dma** has no ROM, so SI and AI timing has no hardware check yet.
- **The golden logs' cart page phase.** It differs from the prebuilt ROM's: the page crossing is at +352 B on hardware and +304 B in our run (inferred from the size of the jump). Sizes 305-352 still pass.
